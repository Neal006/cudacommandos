## 1. Where the score actually goes

A perfect matcher given blocking recall R scores `1.25R / (0.25 + R)`.

| | recall | ceiling | we score | % of ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| this morning | 0.9498 | 0.9903 | 0.9626 | 97.2% |
| now (hopeso) | **0.9630** | **0.9924** | ~0.967 | ~97.4% |

**I got the headline wrong this morning and corrected it this afternoon.** I
said 0.99 was unreachable because recall capped the leaderboard near 0.980.
That was true at recall 0.9498. At 0.9630 the ceiling is 0.9924, and inverting
the formula, the leader's 0.9906 needs only **R >= 0.955** with a near-perfect
matcher. Retrieval stopped being the binding constraint the moment hopeso
landed. The whole remaining gap is matcher precision: we are at ~97.4% of our
ceiling, the leader is at ~99.8% of theirs.

That reframing is the single most important thing learned today, and it is why
four separate recall projects were killed after it.

## 2. What worked

### hopeso (Neal) - the day's one real win

Sibling expansion: find one copy of a business, pull its siblings through
shared `(country, name-key)` pools. Sources hold 5-6 copies of one business.

| | value |
|---|---|
| blocking recall | 0.9498 -> **0.9630** |
| cost | **8.15 new candidates/entity** (train), 8.34 (test) |
| frames | train 66.2M -> 84.2M pairs, test 52.0M -> 66.4M |
| build time | 4.2 min train, 3.7 min test on 192 vCPU |
| stage 1 | 0.9494 -> **0.9526 / 0.9533** |

Compare the retriever union built the same morning: +0.0162 recall for +40%
candidates. hopeso gets +0.0127 for +27%, using the match graph instead of more
TF-IDF passes. Roughly 3x more efficient per candidate.

### The contention fix (run 011, v4)

Stage-2 claim features were computed over the 150k training sample but applied
to the full frame at inference, so `n_claims` meant different things in
training and test.

| | plain (pairs/record) | row-weighted (what the model sees) |
|---|---|---|
| train, sample-only | 1.957 | 10.80 |
| train, full frame | 6.717 | 145.5 |
| **test** | **5.549** | **91.0** |

From 3.5x too low to 1.2x too high on the plain measure; 8.4x to 1.6x
row-weighted. OOF 0.9626 -> **0.9644**, which had been predicted impossible
(the fix targets a mismatch OOF cannot see). It moved anyway, so the
sample-only counts were noisy as well as mis-scaled.

Measuring test's row-weighted contention for the first time today also fixed a
log line that had been comparing a row-weighted mean against a plain one and
reading as a 26x miss.

### Calibration on a test-like holdout

OOF scores each entity with one fold model; test uses the mean of five. The
isotonic calibrator was fitted on the first and applied to the second. Every
cloud run now fits the decision layer on a 50k-entity holdout scored the way
test is, so `holdout_score` is a direct leaderboard predictor with no -0.010
correction.

## 3. What was measured and rejected

Four ideas died today. All four were killed by gates written before the
measurement ran, which is the part of the process that worked.

### Multi-retriever union (experiment 012)

Gate: each pass must add >= +0.002 of F0.5 ceiling.

| pass | recall | ceiling | marginal |
|---|---|---|---|
| a: name+addr word (current) | 0.9497 | 0.9895 | - |
| + b: name only | 0.9564 | 0.9910 | +0.0015 |
| + c: char 3-5gram translit | 0.9648 | 0.9928 | +0.0018 |
| + d: address only | 0.9659 | 0.9930 | +0.0002 |

All three fail. Recall rises a real +0.0162 but F0.5 converts it to +0.0035,
because `1.25R/(0.25+R)` is nearly flat up here, and the union costs +40%
candidates that the matcher pays for in precision.

### Address-anchored keys (experiment 013)

Three keys anchored on the house number, the shape a public 0.976 solution
reports, rebuilt on our own normalizer. They *work* - a Devanagari name keys
identically to its Latin twin - but efficiency decides it:

| pass (top3 cap50) | misses recovered | new/entity | per candidate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cn` (existing) | 1,004 (19.2%) | 5.42 | **185** |
| `sk` (existing) | 772 (14.8%) | 5.20 | **148** |
| `nn` (new) | 685 (13.1%) | 6.07 | 113 |
| `ns` (new) | 508 (9.7%) | 6.35 | 80 |
| `nc` (new) | 235 (4.5%) | 7.76 | 30 |

All three new keys are less efficient than the two we have. Widening caps on
the existing keys fails the same way: the largest union buys +0.0019 of ceiling
for **18.6x** the candidates. The default VIBE is on the efficient frontier.

### Early-stopping patience - proposed here, and wrong

`lgb.early_stopping(100)` watches binary_logloss while the objective is macro
F0.5, and fold `best_iter` ranged 436 to 3,611 on identical data. Two boxes
running the same frame at the same time:

    box a  fold 0: best_iter  473  valid 0.9474
    box e5 fold 0: best_iter 3381  valid 0.9524

This was argued to be worth ~+0.004 and a box was spent on patience 500.
Result: stage 1 **0.9532** against the patience-100 box's **0.9533**.
Individual folds moved a lot (fold 1 went 712 -> 1885, +0.0034 valid) but it
averages out. Box killed, ~$30 saved.

### bge-reranker-v2-m3 - right model, wrong hardware

Apache-2.0, 567,755,777 params (verified against the rules: MIT/Apache, <= 8B).
Fine-tuned cleanly on an A10G: bench 1,081 pairs/s, valid logloss 0.0232,
**AUC 0.99959 against e5-small's 0.99907**. Promoted.

Then the CPU band gate measured **69 pairs/s** on 192 vCPU with AMX - 14.5 h
for the 3.6M band pairs. GPU quota caps at 48 vCPU, too few for the rest of the
pipeline, and narrowing the band to what 69 pairs/s affords makes it narrower
than the 0.2-0.8 band e5 already covers. Aborted at 1.7 minutes and ~$0.30
instead of 14 hours and ~$150.

bge is the better reranker on hardware we do not have.

## 4. The cloud, and two traps in it

### The quota pools are inverted

This cost the most time of anything today.

| | training quota | processing quota |
|---|---|---|
| `ml.m7i.48xlarge`, `ml.c7i.48xlarge` (192 vCPU) | **0** | **2** |
| every `g`-family GPU | **>= 1** | **0** |

So the big CPU boxes must be **Processing** jobs and the GPU box must be a
**Training** job. PIPELINE_98 assumed the opposite for the CPU boxes and
assumed no GPU existed at all. The separate-pools fact had already been written
into `PLAN_WITH_COMPUTE.md` in the morning and was still filtered past at
15:00, concluding the fleet was unrunnable. Priyanshu pushed back and was right.

### A PyTorch container without PyTorch

Two jobs died hours apart on:

    AutoModelForSequenceClassification requires the PyTorch library
    but it was not found in your environment

on a PyTorch image. The 2.3 container ships torch 2.3; `transformers>=4.40`
resolves to 5.x; transformers 5.x rejects torch 2.3 and reports it **absent**
rather than **too old**. Nothing fails at import - the first torch-backed class
on the reranker path is inside stage 2, so a 2-hour r5.24xlarge run did
blocking, stage 1 (0.9517) and most of stage 2 before dying. **~$17 wasted.**

Fixed by matching the container to the local known-good stack (2.5.1/py311 =
torch 2.5.1, transformers 5.17, numpy 2.1) and adding a `preflight()` that
touches the class in the first 60 seconds.

### Other snags, one line each

Cross-account bucket -> server-side copy. `GetObjectTagging` denied ->
`--copy-props none`. SDK v3 dropped `sagemaker.pytorch` -> pin v2. `Session()`
needs an explicit region. `hopeso.build` does not block, it unions onto an
existing base frame and exits without one. A GPU training job returns its model
as `output.tar.gz`, so the CPU box unpacks tarballs in place rather than
round-tripping 1.6 GB through a laptop.

## 8. The decoy-density question (from a public 0.969 solution)

A public solution reports: *"the test set is a disjoint sample of the training
data with twice the decoy density"*, and says it adjusts its threshold for it.
Verified independently from our own counts:

| | train | test | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| S2/S3 records per S1 entity | 4.677 | 5.754 | 1.23x |
| **decoys per entity** (assuming equal true links) | 1.216 | 2.294 | **1.89x** |
| decoy fraction of the pool | 26.0% | 39.9% | |

Their claim is right. But it does **not** convert into a threshold correction,
because blocking absorbs it:

| | train | test | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| candidates per entity after blocking | 38.15 | 38.34 | **1.005x** |
| positive rate among candidates | 8.73% | ~8.69% | ~1.00x |

Top-K is selected per entity. A pool with more decoys does not give an entity
more candidates -- it gives it *better* decoys. The count and the prior the
model actually sees are unchanged, so a prior-shift correction would be
correcting something that is not there.

What remains is a difficulty shift: the decoys that survive into the top-38 are
nearer misses in test than in train. That is a plausible mechanism for the
stable -0.0102 / -0.0097 / -0.0096 offline-to-leaderboard gap, but it gives no
formula, and a guessed threshold nudge is not worth spending one of three
remaining uploads on.

**The empirical substitute:** submit the first candidate early, measure the
actual holdout-to-leaderboard gap, and use that measured number to rank the
remaining two. That obtains the correction instead of assuming it, which is
also why the submission schedule fires at ~18:30 rather than at 23:00.
